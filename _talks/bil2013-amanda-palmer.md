Speaking shortly after her TED talk, Amanda Palmer recaps her path from street-performing living statue to the record-setting Kickstarter campaign for her album, which raised about $1.2 million, and argues that musicians don't need to make fans pay — they need to ask, and to stay connected with their audiences as the industry's old middleman model shifts. She then opens the floor, fielding questions on whether crowdfunding is viable for unknown or introverted artists, how to set a Kickstarter goal, and why couchsurfing-style sharing appeals to her. She closes with a story she cut from her TED talk: how hard it was to ask her husband, writer Neil Gaiman, for a loan after canceling a tour to be with a sick friend, and what learning to accept his help meant to her.
