## Event facts (period sources, verbatim where quoted)

- Site title/tagline: "BIL 2009 — Minds Set Free" (2009.bilconference.com). Rotating backronyms: "Brilliance. Inspiration. Levity." / "Brilliance. Impromptu. Logic." / "Beauty. Ingenuous. Luminary." / "Boisterous. Ingenuous. Levity." / "Breeding. Ingenuous. Life."
- Venue: Cal State Long Beach Student Union (Wired, Feb 7 2009: "It's being held at the Cal State Long Beach Student Union."). Wiki sidebar: "BIL2009 - Feb 7-8, 2009 in Long Beach, CA. Volunteers meet at 8:30am each day in the main room. Saturday: Opening Session 9:30am. Sunday: Opening Session 9:00am."
- Scale: wiki speaker-schedule page: "Over 100 guest speakers. Only two short days! WOWZA". Talk Format post (1/27): "BIL will have probably 80 speakers and 4 rooms." Hands-on room post (1/26): "We just had our 70th speaker sign up." Wired: "This year more than 1,000 people have registered in advance, and conference organizers are expecting at least another 500 will just show up." Retrospective About page: "BIL 2009 was held on the campus of California State University at Long Beach and with over 100 speakers and 500 participants, doubling the size the previous year." (2009-era Vimeo album text says "over 450 participants".)
- Rooms: A (Main, 450 seats, 15-min talks), B (54–55 seats, 30-min), C (20 seats, 45-min discussion), D (20 seats, demo/hands-on "2+2"), plus impromptu "Room F: YOU MAKE IT" (butcher-paper self-scheduling) and an Infinity open-format room per Room Map post. "Downstairs is the pool hall, arcade, and bowling alley."
- Video: Revision3 livestreamed/filmed the main room (http://revision3.com/bil/); UStream carried the whole event (ustream.tv/channel/bil-conference). Community videos collected on wiki page "BIL2009 - Videos" and the Vimeo album 70246.
- Money: free to attend, ~$20/attendee cost; donations to the Singularity Institute (501(c)(3) co-sponsor). Sponsors page lists Cernio Technology Co-operative + individual donors (Alicia Isaac, Grant Patterson, Ryan Arroyo, Brian Urbanik, Matt Knopp & Alexis Bright, Tom Heaps-Nelson, Debra Druther, Darin Douma, Daniel Gould).
- Scheduling method: "Fav" voting on bil.nowgetsocial.com with locally-linear-embedding clustering by Rion Snow (fav-talks post, 1/19).
- Organizers/hosts on schedule: "Bill & Reichart & Todd Huffman — Welcome to BIL"; Reichart (Von Wolfsheild) emceed openings/wraps. Repo event page lists organizers Cody Marx Bailey, Bill Erickson, Todd Huffman.

### "Why you should 'Fav' Talks" — Todd Huffman, January 19, 2009 (excerpt)
> Last year we received feedback it was 'hard to see everything'. This is of course impossible, there are over 50 talks scheduled, but we're trying to cluster talks based on user interests to maximize the amount of content everybody can see. …
> A note on the methods used, which were designed by Rion Snow "…this one is based on treating each talk as a 137-dimensional vector (the number of unique voters), and creating a 2D embedding of that 137-D space that tries to preserve local distances between neighbors. The technique is "locally linear embedding"."
> These techniques are giving groupings such as: The Middle East - The Great Vanishing – Outworking & Innovation — Financial Consequences of Longevity – Robot Cars – Towards Real AI — Crowdsourcing the Response – Oh yeah, it's the People – Freedom isn't Free – Working with Complex Systems – Your Energy Identity – Greed, Power, and Sex-Life of Pixels

Comments: bmchenry (01.21.09): "Would like to request that my talk on \"Zars: Cars that form Trains\" be scheduled next to Brad Templeton's talk on Robocars." — Todd (01.21.09): "@bmchenry – Sound good, we'll be manually curating the schedule a bit when we see obvious connections." — hardaway (01.31.09, on Talk Format post): "How do I know if I am still on the schedule? … WOuld like to talk about starting a company in a downturn."

### "Hands-on room" — Todd Huffman, January 26, 2009
> We just had our 70th speaker sign up. Super 'Hello World' Experience. The presenter, Joseph Adams, will be doing hands-on 1-on-1 tutorials which will allow you to run your own software on Arizona State University's Saguaro high-performance computer (even if you are a noob, he'll start with "Hello World"). What do you have to say in four trillion computations per second (two teraflops)?
> Which brings me to the point… Joseph and others are doing 'hands-on' demos rather than talks. BIL will have a room where you can bring your awesome art or technology and show it to others. So it looks like we'll have people from the San Diego Fab Lab, MindShare, ASU High-Performance Computing, SDSU VizLab, and more.

### "Talk Format" — Todd Huffman, January 27, 2009 (excerpt)
> BIL will have probably 80 speakers and 4 rooms. We need to spread the speakers between the rooms. There the Main Room with 450 seats, the Middle Room with 55 seats, and two Small Rooms with 20 seats. … Main room – 15 minute talks. Middle room – 30 minute talks. Small room 1 – Flexible time, not to exceed 45 minutes. Small room 2 – Demo Room.

### "Room Map" — Todd Huffman, February 4, 2009
> The floor layout of BIL is important. There are a total of 5 rooms (plus a snack area). Talks in the 15 room (shown below in Red) will be 15 minutes long, very intense, no Q&A. In the 30 room (Purple) talks will be 30 minutes, more interactive, more details. The 45 room (shown in Turquoise) has talks that are, you guessed it, 45 minutes long, and will be more discussion/debate oriented. The Infinity room (Blue) is open format, not sure exactly how this is going to work out. The 2+2 room (Green) is the demo area for tech and art exhibits.

### Wired, "Where TED Goes, BIL Goes" — Kim Zetter, Feb 7, 2009 (https://www.wired.com/2009/02/where-ted-goes/)
> Anyone who couldn't afford the $6,000 price tag to attend the Technology, Entertainment and Design conference this year (aka TED), can still hobnob with entrepreneurs and geniuses at the younger, rowdier BIL conference, being held in Long Beach following the end of TED.
> Launched last year as the "unconference" alternative to TED, BIL organizers piggybacked on the TED concept of gathering smart people from any discipline to share big ideas. But unlike TED, there's no fee to attend and anyone can give a presentation, as long as the audience wants to hear it. The conference even attracts TED speakers and attendees.
> The conference has grown measurably since its first informal gathering in Monterey, California, last year when 200 people registered and 400 showed up, according to conference organizers. This year more than 1,000 people have registered in advance, and conference organizers are expecting at least another 500 will just show up. The conference moved down to Long Beach with TED this year and will begin today and run through Sunday. It's being held at the Cal State Long Beach Student Union.
