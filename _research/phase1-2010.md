# Phase 1 — BIL 2010 (Long Beach, CA — Museum of Latin American Art, Feb 12–14, 2010)

Researched 2026-08-23. Sources: Wayback Machine captures of `2010.bilconference.com` (raw `id_` fetches saved under `tmp/wb2010/`), repo press stubs in `_press/`, the two linked press articles (fetched), and YouTube playlist "BIL: The Early Years (2008-2011)" (`PLCZHPSerQfFeqnQkp_fboBPS3wDkD6Cfm`).

Key facts from the period site itself:

- Venue: Museum of Latin American Art (MOLAA), Long Beach, CA. Dates advertised Feb 12–14 ("moving the event forward a day" per roundup; talks Fri + Sat morning, BILxOutside small-group activities Sat afternoon/Sunday).
- Official roundup (posted Feb 16, 2010): **Speakers: 30, Attendees: 200, Livestream simultaneous: 1,000, Livestream total: 50,000.**
- Three talk formats: "the Fire Hose" (20-minute talks, no Q&A), **JIL** (satellite conference "curated for a feminine perspective", own space in the MOLAA sculpture garden), and the Open Culture Space.
- The site's Speakers category (`/category/speakers/`, single page, no pagination) lists 44 announced talks by 42 named speakers. The roundup's "30 speakers" implies not every announced talk was delivered; delivery is separately evidenced below where possible.
- Sponsors named in period text: Microsoft, Google SideWiki, TechZulu.
- Site tagline rotated backronyms ("Benevolence. Illuminating. Levity." / "Bilateral. Impromptu. Learning." / "Boisterous. Impromptu. Level." / "Breeding. Ingenuous. Level.").

### Bruno Bowden – Browser Size: The Web on a Small Screen (Feb 9, 2010)
Source: http://2010.bilconference.com/2010/02/bruno-bowden-browser-size-the-web-on-a-small-screen/

> **About the Talk**
> Most web sites are designed on large screens, where they look and function very well. Most of your web site visitors though have much smaller screen. This means that critical part of the web page aren't visible to users without scrolling. Many users will get confused if they can't see the important part of a page and will exit rather than scroll downwards.
>
> The technology was used on the Google Earth download page to increase the install rate by 10%. The simple change required was moving the "Install" button 100 pixels upwards.
>
> **About the Speaker**
> Bruno is an Engineer at Google, working on GMail spam. Prior to that he worked at the startup Keyhole which became Google Earth. Browser Size is a 20% project of his at Google.

### Eric Boyd – Wearable Electronic Senses (Feb 4, 2010)
Source: http://2010.bilconference.com/2010/02/eric-boyd-wearable-electronic-senses/

> **About the Talk**
> The North Paw is a compass anklet: it vibrates on your ankle to tell you which way is north. Because the stimulus is persistent, your brain adapts to the device, and you acquire an intuitive sense of north.
>
> Sensebridge is a company I have started (with some friends) that aims to explore the space of wearable electronic senses. We bridge the world you can't feel into the senses you already have: sensebridge. The North Paw bridges a magnetic sense, we also have projects to bridge ultrasonic sound (and thereby echolocation), heart beat, and "eyes in the back of your back": feeling when things are coming up behind you.
>
> Our approach is not visual: although vision is the most powerful of our senses, it's also the most over-burdened already, and is actively used. We feel that the sense of touch offers the most possibilities for augmentation of new sensory stimulus.
>
> If you're interested in wearable electronics or sensory augmentation (aka augmented reality), come to my talk. I'll give a short talk on wearable electronic senses, and then I'll moderate a group discussion / Q & A session. I'll also have a variety of devices on hand that you can try on! Check out our website [sensebridge.net]
>
> **About the Speaker**
> Eric is an engineer and hacker, transhumanist and environmentalist. Lately he's been building wearable electronic devices which augment his senses. Please join the noisebridge cyborg group: 1pm Sundays at Noisebridge (a San Francisco hackerspace), till the hacking stops.
