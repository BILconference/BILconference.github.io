# BIL 2008 — Line-up Research (Phase 1)

Researched 2026-08-23. Sources: Wayback Machine captures of bilconference.com (2008), two period WIRED articles (fetched via Wayback), Kevin Kelly's kk.org preview post (live), YouTube playlist "BIL: The Early Years (2008-2011)", repo press stubs, `former_speakers.md`.

**Location correction:** every period source places BIL 2008 in **Monterey, CA** (March 1-2, 2008, alongside TED Monterey — TED did not move to Long Beach until 2009). WIRED's news article carries a "MONTEREY, California" dateline; Kevin Kelly wrote "an unconference that will take place in Monterey, CA"; BIL's own 2008-03-03 blog post says "BIL2009 will be in Long Beach **next** year with TED." The repo's `_events/bil-2008.md` already agrees (venue: El Estero Park Complex, 777 Pearl Street, Monterey). The "Santa Monica / TED Long Beach" framing in the research brief is wrong for 2008.

Fetched raw HTML is preserved in `tmp/wayback2/` (plus `tmp/test.html` for the Garrett Lisi post).

## Period text (verbatim)

All bilconference.com text below is from raw (`id_`) Wayback captures of the original WordPress 2.5.1 pages; HTML entities decoded (&#8217; = ', &#8212; = —, &#8216; = ', typos preserved as printed).

### A. Garrett Lisi
Source: https://web.archive.org/web/20080516203402/http://bilconference.com/2008/01/24/a-garrett-lisi/

> **An Exceptionally Simple Theory of Everything**
>
> In his much talked about paper *An Exceptionally Simple Theory of Everything,* physicist Garrett Lisi proposed a novel model for all fundamental interactions ever observed.  At BIL Garrett will be fielding questions and discussing everything and anything.

(Photo: /wp-content/themes/bil/images/aglisi.jpg)

### Aileen Mapes
Source: https://web.archive.org/web/20080516203617/http://bilconference.com/2008/01/24/aileen-mapes/

> **Short Film Festival**
>
> Choreographer and filmmaker Aileen Mapes will be curating a short film festival comprising of original submissions as well as her personal favourites.  Pieces will be accompanied by lively discussion, insight, and opinion.

(Photo: /wp-content/themes/bil/images/aileen.jpg)

### Bill Erickson & Cody Marx Bailey
Source: https://web.archive.org/web/20080516203509/http://bilconference.com/2008/01/24/bill-erickson-cody-marx-bailey/

> **Coworking to Coliving — Digital Utopia?**
>
> Humanity has always searched for paradise, and the dream of utopia has seeded many unconventional life-structures over the millennium.  In this presentation participants in the 'Creative Space' project discuss the successes, challenges and future of another attempt to create a utopian community, one with a Web Two Point Oh spin.  Come prepared to contribute your thoughts and ideas on intentional communities, entrepreneurialism, and social technologies.

(Photo: /wp-content/themes/bil/images/creativespace.jpg)

### Deb Lavoy
Source: https://web.archive.org/web/20080516203316/http://bilconference.com/2008/01/24/deb-lavoy/

> **Keeping Context**
>
> Context - for years we've been searching for information, finding it, using it, sharing and bookmarking it - but how we found it and how we used it is at least as interesting and useful, as what we found. We're throwing it all that context away - but we don't have to.  Save the context!

(No photo in post.)

### Jason Youn
Source: https://web.archive.org/web/20080516203402/http://bilconference.com/2008/01/24/jason-youn/ (raw capture in tmp/wayback/tmp.html; nearest-snapshot fetch)

> **Photography for the Common Man?**
>
> Radical drops in the cost of consumer electronics have brought the artistic tools of photography to the masses.  Professional photographer Jason Youn will be discussing the philosophy of the photographer as an observer and participant, as well as the impact of photography theory on techniques which help the amateur further blur the lines between vacation photos and fine art.

(Photo: /wp-content/themes/bil/images/jasonyoun.jpg)

### Marshall Reeves
Source: https://web.archive.org/web/20080516203532/http://bilconference.com/2008/01/24/marshall-reeves/

> **Digital Graffiti: Art, Advertising, and Anarchy**
>
> As the world becomes increasing electronic, the legal definitions of property, public space, and damage change with respect to criminality. The lack of physicality or permanace to many acts potentially labeled as graffiti such as paintless light graffiti (pictured), underground advertising, and nondestructive hacking all blur the borders of legality around the world. Although presently underground, these ways of expressing ideas will undoubtedly surface in the coming years as artistic, corporate, and anarchistic machines. The legislative responses to digital property and electronic footprints in public spaces will shape the vista of tomorrow.

(Photo: /wp-content/themes/bil/images/marshall.jpg. Note original typos: "increasing electronic", "permanace".)

### Todd Huffman
Source: https://web.archive.org/web/20080516203339/http://bilconference.com/2008/01/24/todd-huffman/

> **Hacking the Human Fantastic**
>
> The boundaries between the human and his environment have been thinning for thousands of years, and this session will trace the co-evolution of the human mind and the technology it has created.  From the cuniform and cartouch to the iLife, the impact and significance of artifacts on the human experience are often not recognized for what they are, implements of dehumanization.  The subtle cyborgation of the modern consumer will be discussed in with respect to our designs, desires, and destiny.

(Photo: /wp-content/themes/bil/images/todd.jpg. Note original typos: "cuniform and cartouch", "discussed in with respect".)

### Aubrey de Grey
Source: https://web.archive.org/web/20080516203447/http://bilconference.com/2008/01/27/aubrey-de-grey/

> **How to Be a Successful Heretic**
>
> In my 12 minutes I will give a light-hearted - but unerringly accurate - string of nuggets of advice, drawn mostly from my own experience of recent years, on how to shepherd a diabolically dangerous idea from conception to world domination (yes, the last part will be drawn from others' experience!) as quickly as possible in the teeth of determined and powerful vested interests. My comments will apply not only to science but to heresies in general.

(Photo: /wp-content/themes/bil/images/aubrey.jpg. Written in de Grey's first person.)

### Baron RK Von Wolfsheild
Source: https://web.archive.org/web/20080516203424/http://bilconference.com/2008/01/27/baron-rk-von-wolfsheild/

> **Darknets: fascist gated associations, or intentional community?**
>
> A darknet is a private virtual network where people connect only to others they trust. The web is being built in our own image; we bring to it our fears, and our hopes. Some communities are designed to promote social interaction, others are designed to protect and to segregate. Even if the internet were made available to every person, would any services be available? Where do features and ethics collide?

(Photo: /wp-content/themes/bil/images/baron.jpg)

### Quinn Norton
Source: https://web.archive.org/web/20080516203637/http://bilconference.com/2008/01/27/quinn-norton/ (also present verbatim as the commented-out "2008 Featured Speaker" block in https://web.archive.org/web/20080509163019/http://bilconference.com/)

> **Always the Next Human**
>
> Society, technology, and bodies are all breaking each other. In the coming decades, we are likely to be pushing out the boundaries of our definition of human. Body modification, pharmaceuticals, the justice system, medical research, class systems, psychology and surgery are swept up in the same wave- how we see our selves, and how we act upon our bodies. The next humans will need new ethics, new markets, new laws, and very different criteria for choosing research goals and legal solutions. It can get very scary, but don't worry too much- turns out we've done all this before, though not this fast. We've done ok so far. Let's figure out how to do it better this time.

(Photo: /wp-content/themes/bil/images/quinn.jpg)

## Period press (verbatim key passages)

### WIRED: "Informal Conference Borrows TED's Big Ideas, Not Its Pretense" — by Quinn Norton, 03.03.08
Original: http://www.wired.com/techbiz/people/news/2008/03/bil — archived raw: https://web.archive.org/web/20090110234041/http://www.wired.com/techbiz/people/news/2008/03/bil (local: tmp/wayback2/wired-bil-article.html, full text)

Photo caption:
> Aubrey de Grey answers questions about his work in life extension during a Q&A session after his talk on heresy at the BIL conference. *Quinn Norton/Wired.com*

Key passages:
> MONTEREY, California -- After controversial physicist Garrett Lisi presented his unorthodox "exceptionally simple theory of everything" at the prestigious Technology, Entertainment and Design (TED) conference, he drove across town to hobnob at BIL, a decidedly more downscale "unconference" also held here.
>
> "I packed up the car, left the fancy hotel, moved to the cheap hotel, and showed up at 3:30," Lisi said. He was immediately mobbed by the University of California Santa Cruz Physics Department, most of which had carpooled to BIL in the morning.

> Topics at BIL ranged from Aubrey de Grey's efforts to extend life indefinitely, a talk on gifted education by an educator who also went through the process, one man's experience as a foreign aid coordinator in Africa and a quick lesson in the state of the art in stem cells.

> To BIL presenter Chris Phoenix it was clear that the association with TED raised people's expectations on the quality and profundity of the talks. "Speakers held themselves to high standards," he explained. Phoenix himself presented on several subjects, including a possible cause for the Cambrian explosion.

> BIL organizer Todd Huffman estimates that around 10 percent of the BIL crowd came over from TED.

> BIL's other famous crossover speaker, Aubrey de Grey, spoke at TED two years ago about his field of radical life extension. This year he spoke to BIL attendees about "how to be a successful heretic," something he knows quite well from his controversial defiance of the conventions of gerontology.

> To Huffman, BIL was a success in part because "We almost had too many people ... which was perfect." Also, he added after a pause, "We were not arrested or kicked out."
>
> Next year BIL is moving to Long Beach, and like TED, is looking for a bigger venue.
